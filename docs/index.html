An agent-based model of two-round elections

What makes voters abandon their favourite candidate?

In a two-round election, a voter who sees that nobody they can tolerate is on course to reach the runoff may switch to someone they like less. This model follows that choice for thousands of voters at once, while the polls and the votes pull on each other.

A research prototype built for a master's thesis (ENS-PSL / Centre Borelli). The mechanism is under revision, so nothing on this page should be read as a finding.

How it works

One round, for every voter

The model covers the first round only. The runoff is never simulated: it exists as the voters' guess about who will qualify for it.

Read the poll

Everyone sees the same public poll and mixes it with their own prior belief about who is ahead.

List who is tolerable

Each voter can put up with the candidates within a distance τ of their own position, their favourite included.

Guess the runoff

Each voter takes the two candidates they believe are strongest as the likely finalists.

Stay or switch

If a tolerable candidate is among them, the voter stays sincere. If none is, they may switch, but only if the gain outweighs the cost μ of leaving their favourite.

↻ The new vote intentions feed the next poll, and the loop starts again.

Model

The decision rule

Voters and candidates sit on a left-right axis from −1 to 1. Candidates do not act. Each voter a has a position xa, a favourite j* (the candidate they vote for sincerely), and a belief about how strong each candidate is.

1 · Belief update

At each round the voter mixes a fixed prior πa with the public poll st. The weight α sets how much they trust their own prior over the poll.

mat = α · πa + (1 − α) · st

2 · Who is tolerable

The contender set holds every candidate within distance τ. It always includes the favourite.

Ca = { j : |xa − xj| ≤ τ }

The sliders use τ̂, the tolerance measured in "candidate spacings": with K candidates, τ = τ̂ × 2/K. That keeps the value comparable between elections with different numbers of candidates. The conversion happens in exactly one place in the code.

3 · The trigger

The voter projects the two candidates with the highest belief as the finalists TR. They have a strategic incentive only when none of their tolerable candidates is among them:

Ga = 1 ⇔ Ca ∩ TR = ∅

4 · Switch or stay

A triggered voter picks, among tolerable candidates, the one with the best score: how viable it is locally, times its projected runoff share, minus a cost for leaving the favourite.

φa(j) = LVa,j · NVa,j,k* − μ · λ̂a(j)
LVa,jlocal viability: the belief in j, renormalised over the tolerable set
NVa,j,k*projected runoff share of j against the strongest opponent k*
λ̂a(j)expressive cost: how much less the voter likes j than their favourite (zero for the favourite)
μloyalty weight. At μ = 0 the choice is purely instrumental; a large μ pins the voter to their favourite
The new vote intentions become the next poll (in the imagined electorate) and the loop repeats, 25 rounds here. In the France mode the polls are the real weekly ones instead.

Why 2002 and 2022

2002 is the textbook coordination failure: the French left split its vote across several candidates and its front-runner missed the runoff. 2022 is the contrasting case, with visible consolidation around a few candidates.

One behavioural setting is applied to both years and only the environment changes. Nothing is fitted to either election; where the model differs from the real result, the difference is recorded as it is.

Outcomes

How coordination is measured

Effective number of candidates

ENP(δ) = 1 / Σj δj²

A vote split evenly across 4 candidates gives ENP = 4; a vote concentrated on one gives 1. When voters coordinate, ENP falls. The "fall in the effective number of candidates" above is ENP of the sincere vote minus ENP of the final vote.

CENP, the version comparable across elections

CENP = (K − ENP) / (K − 1)

It runs from 0 (maximal fragmentation) to 1 (full coordination), so elections with different numbers of candidates K can be put side by side.

Switching

Trigger rateshare of voters for whom no tolerable candidate is projected to qualify
Switching rateshare of all voters who end up away from their favourite
Conditional switchingshare of triggered voters who actually switch

A high trigger rate with low conditional switching means the incentive exists, but the cost of leaving a favourite holds people back.

All outcome measures, including the two ΔCENP baselines: docs/model.md#outcome-measures

Validation

How the code is checked

The test suite runs on every push and pull request, and numerical warnings count as failures: an overflow or a divide-by-zero inside the model fails the build. It exists because an earlier round of empirical results was invalidated by a unit-conversion error that every figure had looked plausible under.

Is the code doing what the equations say?

  • Hand-computed values. Metrics match closed-form results on tiny inputs, to 1e-12.
  • The decision rule. Hand-built voters check that the trigger fires exactly when no tolerable candidate is projected to qualify, and that the choice flips at the analytically derived value of μ.
  • Invariants at every round. Every voter is counted once, shares sum to 1, the projected finalists are two distinct valid candidates.
  • Symmetry. Relabelling the candidates, or mirroring the axis left to right, must not change any decision. These were derived from the equations before being tested; all 120 relabellings hold.
  • Golden regressions. Full output vectors for two fixed runs are pinned to 1e-12, so a change that silently moves every number fails.
  • Data integrity. Candidate positions, polls and results stay aligned across files, since everything is indexed by position.

Is the simulation protocol sound?

  • Population size. 2 000 voters is where the coordination measure stops moving across seeds.
  • Number of rounds. Averages are robust to the 25-round ceiling, even though single runs with very spread-out electorates keep oscillating.
  • Robustness panels A–G and a global sensitivity analysis (Sobol indices, 30 720 runs) on 8 parameters.
  • Seed noise. How much variation is randomness rather than parameters, with multiple-testing correction.

Is it reproducible?

  • Every run is bit-identical for a fixed seed.
  • Derived tables regenerate byte-for-byte, checked by comparing file bytes, not parsed numbers.
  • Runs refuse to overwrite existing output without a flag, and interrupted sweeps resume to identical results.
  • This page's data are re-checked against the model by a test, so the page cannot drift from the code.

Reproduce

Run it yourself

Install

git clone https://github.com/clarasalas/strategic-voting-abm-2RS.git
cd strategic-voting-abm-2RS
pip install -e .

One simulation

from core_model.model import run_simulation
from core_model.metrics import tau_absolute, enp

K = 8
r = run_simulation(K=K, n_modes=1, width_factor=1.5, theta=1.0, rho=100.0,
                   rho_pi=100.0, n_electors=500, tau=tau_absolute(1.75, K),
                   mu=0.1, alpha_prior=0.0, K_runoff=2, max_iterations=15,
                   seed=42, verbose=False, collect_diagnostics=True)
print(enp(r["sincere_shares"]), "->", enp(r["final_shares"]))

This prints 5.441 → 5.208 (19 of 500 voters switch). Those exact numbers are pinned by a test.

Tests

pip install pytest && pip install -e ".[analysis]"
python -m pytest -ra

python demo/precompute.py --force   # about 40 minutes, writes docs/data/grid.json

Data, limits and what is next

Data

  • Results: Ministère de l'Intérieur.

What the model leaves out

Only the first round is simulated. There is no campaign, no turnout decision or abstention, and no candidate entering or leaving the race. Every voter sees the same poll; there is no private information.

Where it stands

The empirical replay was re-run in full (14 000 simulations) on 2026-08-21, after a tolerance-unit defect was found and corrected. The analysis is in progress toward an article and the mechanism is under revision, so the repository makes no claims about what the model shows.
